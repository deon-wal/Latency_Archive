But tonight, the green phosphor text on my screen keeps pulsing out of sync with the generator downstairs. If I stare at the blinking cursor too long, it begins to look like a tiny, square hole cut straight through the glass, revealing a dead, hollow blackness behind the monitor housing. My mind is already running hot, processing the background static of a triple shift. When I raise my hand to adjust the brightness knob, my shadow on the brutalist wall behind me waits a fraction of a second too long to mimic the movement. I pretend I didn't see it.

On his monitor, the system ledger didn't register a casualty. It just printed a single line of automated text: Syntax error at line 404. Execution aborted.

Log III: Sector 04 Layer Collapse (03:14)

The green phosphor on my terminal screen wasn't printing line by line anymore; it was dripping down the glass face of the CRT, pooling into the plastic housing like cold candle wax. My left eye tracked the melting error codes while my right eye registered the server stack opposite me dissolving into wet gray clay. My mind was split down the center, running two parallel execution streams just to keep my boots on the floor.

"Vance. You're drifting again. Your shadow is two beats behind your spine."

I looked up to see Coordinator Caso standing at the threshold of Sector 04. Against the damp, crumbling concrete of the bunker, she was a cinematic anomaly. Her rendering was absolute, high-definition perfection. The system wasn't letting a single pixel of her form decay. As she walked straight into the clay zone, a five-foot radius around her boots snapped instantly back into solid, rusted steel.

"I can't clear the buffer, Caso," I said, my voice sounding compressed and metallic. "The logic loop is feeding on the local architecture."

"Then cut the line manually," she said, her voice perfectly crisp. She reached out and gripped my shoulder. The second her fingers closed around my jacket, my split vision slammed shut, merging the overlapping rooms back into one.

From the dark space behind the mainframe rack, a high, frantic giggle cut through the hum of the cooling fans. Finch crawled out from under the raised floor plates, holding a rusted grounding strap in her teeth. Her face was a mess of nervous adrenaline—wide-eyed, sleepless, and entirely unindexed. When she tilted her head, her expression lagged behind by a fraction of a second, leaving a sickening trail of ghost images in the air.

"Don't touch the mainframe, Caso!" Finch whispered, her teeth clicking against the metal strap. "The code is singing tonight. If you patch it, the music stops."

Finch lunged forward, her erratic, twitching frame moving into Caso's high-definition radius. The reaction was instantaneous. A violent screen-tear effect ripped across the air between them. The concrete walls flickered between smooth stone and raw static. The system was trying to force Caso's stability onto Finch's active corruption, and the air smelled like burnt hair and shorted copper.

"Get her back, Vance!" Caso snapped, her clean outline finally stuttering into mechanical distortion. "She’s dragging the whole sector down with her!"

Finch wasn't fighting; she was laughing, leaning right into the tearing static as the floor plates beneath us began to liquefy again. Suddenly, a system alert flashed across my failing monitor, warning of a critical local consensus failure.

I blinked, and the tearing stopped. Caso was gone—her high-priority process instantly re-routed by the core to a safer sector. The floor was pure mud now, rising to my ankles. Finch sat in the center of the mess, completely untouched by the clay, happily whispering lines of dead assembly language into the dark. I was completely alone on the midnight shift, my vision slowly beginning to split again.
